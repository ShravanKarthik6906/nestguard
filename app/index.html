The problem

The biggest risks are invisible from the ground.

A pilot standing in a field sees blue sky. They cannot see the gusts at 120 feet, the cloud base dropping below the FAA limit, the red-tailed hawk that defends this tree line, the migrating flock passing overhead, or the power line hidden against the trees. Each of those can bring a drone down, and a falling drone can hurt people and wildlife.

1,500 eggs were abandoned at Bolsa Chica, California, on May 13, 2021, after a drone crashed into a nesting colony of terns and the birds left their nests. A single check before that flight would have said Don't fly. Replay that check

How it works

Ten hazards, checked together, one answer.

01 Pick a spot and a time. Tap the map, use your location, or let the controller device send its GPS position.

02 NestGuard checks every hazard at once. Weather at drone height, FAA weather minimums, birds, power lines and people below.

03 You get Go, Wait or Don't fly. With the reason, the best time to fly instead, and a spoken alert so your eyes stay on the drone.

Wind and rain. Wind and gusts at drone height, and rain, from Open-Meteo. Cloud base and visibility. The FAA Part 107 minimums: 3 statute miles of visibility and 500 feet below the clouds. Raptors. Recent hawk and eagle sightings from eBird and iNaturalist, because they attack drones. Nesting and migration. Nesting season, colonies nearby, and spring and fall migration activity. Power lines and people below. The distance to the nearest line and how built up the area is, from OpenStreetMap. Air quality. Smoke and haze that cut visibility.

    The device

    A small box clipped to the controller.

    It reads the same answer every five seconds, lights up green, yellow or red, and flashes its screen when the pilot gets close to a risky zone. It also reports temperature and light, so the app can show that it is online. For indoor demos the app walks a pretend path and the device follows it.

    Privacy

    We never show where birds nest.

    Exact nest and sighting locations stay on our server. The map only shows zones blurred to a grid of about 1 km, so nobody can use NestGuard to find nests. We keep no accounts and never store your exact position. Read the privacy page

    Built with

    MongoDB Atlas holds the blurred bird zones and answers the "near me" search. Tiger Data keeps sighting trends, device history and the check counts. Google Gemini writes one plain sentence explaining each answer. Fish Audio speaks the alerts. Vultr runs the app and the API on one server over https. The data comes from eBird, iNaturalist, Open-Meteo and OpenStreetMap.
